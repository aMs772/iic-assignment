What is This Institute Body All About? 
If you’re seeing “IIC” for the first time and wondering what it is - you’re not alone. Some students at IIT Bombay, 
mostly fresher’s don’t know about it, and that’s exactly why this document exists. 

The Institution’s Innovation Council (IIC) is a government-mandated institute body that exists inside every higher 
education institution in India. It was established in November 2018 by the Ministry of Education through its 
Innovation Cell (MIC) at AICTE headquarters. Unlike student clubs, IIC is a formal part of the institute’s 
administrative structure - it reports through Deputy Director (ART) directly to the Ministry of Education. In IIT 
Bombay IIC started operating in 2022. 

Think of it this way: IIC is to innovation what NAAC is to academic quality - a nationally standardised, measurably 
accountable framework that ensures every college in India has a functioning innovation ecosystem.

Why Your Participation Matters 
IIC is not just another body on campus - it is the official channel through which IIT Bombay’s innovation activities 
are reported to the Government of India. When you participate in IIC activities, your work gets nationally 
documented, contributes to IIT Bombay’s NIRF Innovation ranking, and connects you to government programmes 
worth real funding.

• Free Patent Filing via KAPILA - Get patent filing charges reimbursed up to ₹5,600 per application. Any 
student or faculty member whose innovation merits a patent can benefit directly.

Get visibility, mentoring, and potential funding for prototypes at TRL 4+. 

• IDE Bootcamps - Five-day immersive bootcamps on design thinking, rapid prototyping, and business

• Mentorship & Industry Access - Connect with alumni entrepreneurs, industry experts, and Innovation 
Ambassadors through IIC’s structured networks. 

• Build Your Innovation Portfolio - Your participation is documented on a government portal. This 
strengthens your CV nationally - not just within IIT Bombay. 

• Startup Support Pathway - From ideation workshops to IPR guidance to pre-incubation referrals at SINE, 
IIC facilitates complete pipeline from idea to market.

What Does IIC Actually Do? 
Every IIC operates on a yearly calendar divided into four quarters (Sep through Aug). Activities fall into five 
categories, each with specific weightage: 

Pillar 1: IIC Calendar Activities (40% of Score) 

Prescribed by MIC in the annual activity calendar. Includes design thinking workshops, IPR awareness sessions under 
KAPILA, entrepreneurship development programmes, proof-of-concept and idea pitching sessions, field visits to 
incubators and maker spaces, and inter-institutional innovation competitions. 

Pillar 2: MIC-Driven Activities (10% of Score) 

Scheduled by MIC and mandatory for all IICs. The weightage for MIC-driven activities has been reduced to 10% in 
the current framework. Includes the India First Leadership Talk Series, Innovation Ambassador training, IIC Regional 
Meets across eight zones, and national campaigns like Viksit Bharat@2047. 

Pillar 3: Self-Driven Activities (30% of Score) 

Independently planned by each IIC to suit their campus context. Hackathons tailored to local problems, industry 
mentoring sessions, startup demo days, alumni entrepreneur talks, innovation showcases, and field research 
expeditions. We are working on many initiatives including an AI Innovation chatbot, innovation magazine, single 
innovation calender for the campus, etc. 

Pillar 4: R&D, Sell, and Idea Cafe (10% of Score) 

Dedicated 10% weightage for R&D initiatives, Sell (commercialisation activities), and Idea Cafe - structured 
platforms for research outputs, pitching and selling innovations, and open idea-sharing sessions fostering cross-
disciplinary collaboration. 

Pillar 5: Celebration Day Activities (10% of Score) 

Observing nationally significant days: National Startup Day (Jan 16), World IP Day (Apr 26), National Science Day 
(Feb 28), and National Youth Day (Jan 12). 

 

IIC 8.0 Quarterly Themes (2025–26) 

Q1: Awareness & Idea Generation  |  Q2: Skill Development  |  Q3: Prototyping & Business Model 
Development  |  Q4: Startup Ecosystem & Scale-up. Activities graded Level 1 (awareness) to Level 4

How IIC Maps Events at IIT Bombay 
For Calendar and Self-Driven Activities, IIC IIT Bombay does not operate in isolation. We collaborate with different 
student clubs, student bodies, and institute bodies to map their events with our IIC calendar and report them to 
the Ministry of Education. When a student body conducts a hackathon, workshop, or innovation event, IIC works 
with them to ensure it gets formally documented and reported at the national level. 

This collaborative model ensures that innovation happening across IIT Bombay - whether from E-Cell, a technical 
club, a department, or any other body - is captured, credited, and made visible on the national stage. 

 

“Because IIC is an institute body, everything that IIC reports to the Ministry of Education 
represents IIT Bombay. Therefore, all student clubs, student bodies, and institute bodies should 
make sure that all of their events are reported to the Ministry of Education through IIC. All bodies 
should follow the guidelines given by the Ministry of Education and help IIC in reporting the 

same.” 

- Professor Milind Atrey 
Deputy Director (Academic & Research Transition), IIT Bombay - Stated at the IIC Quarterly Meeting, February 2026 

 
This directive makes it clear: IIC is the official channel through which IIT Bombay’s innovation activities reach the 
Ministry of Education. Every student body and institute body has a role to play. 

 

How Can You Get Involved? 
Whether you’re a student, a club head, or a faculty member, there are concrete ways to engage with IIC: 

• If you’re a student: Participate in IIC-coordinated events like SIH, Hackathons, YUKTI submissions, and 
Celebration Day activities. Register your innovations on the YUKTI portal. File patents through KAPILA. 

• If you run a student club or body: Coordinate with IIC to map your innovation-related events to the IIC 
calendar. This gets your events nationally documented and contributes to IIT Bombay’s NIRF Innovation 
ranking. The upper management of IIT Bombay looks at which body is contributing the most to IIC in 
reporting innovation activities and gets rewarded. We can also help you with room bookings, publicity, 
getting access to experts, etc. 

• If you’re a faculty member: Become a certified Innovation Ambassador through MIC’s training 
programme. Use KAPILA for patent filing support. Report your innovation activities through IIC. 

• If you’re a department or centre: Ensure your workshops, hackathons, and innovation activities are 
reported through IIC to the Ministry of Education. Every event counts.
